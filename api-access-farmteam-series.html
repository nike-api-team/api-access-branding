The moment

Closing the Endurance Gap.

Mountain and trail running has grown over 230% in the last decade. ACG is re-launched. Outdoor brands are crossing from trail to road, and running brands are crossing from road to trail. The time is now for Nike.

The science we know

The Big Three

For decades, endurance running performance has been explained by three physiological pillars — the Joyner Model:

1
VO₂ Max
Maximal oxygen uptake — the ceiling of aerobic capacity
2
Lactate Threshold
The fractional utilization of VO₂max before metabolic accumulation
3
Economy / Efficiency
The oxygen cost of running at a given speed

From the 800 to the marathon, these three variables explain performance with remarkable precision. The relative contributions shift with distance — anaerobic capacity fades, aerobic dominance grows — but the model holds.

Marathon & Below
Economy 50% Threshold 45% VO₂ Max 5%
The Big Three explain performance.
Beyond Marathon (Ultra)
Resilience 60% Threshold 20% Economy 20%
Resilience dominates. The Big Three shrink.

Until it doesn’t.

The science we don’t

The Fourth Dimension

Beyond approximately 2 hours of exercise, the Big Three are no longer static. They deteriorate. VO₂max drifts. Economy worsens. Threshold shifts. The “start line” values that predicted your marathon time are not the values you hold at hour four, eight, or sixteen.

Physiological Resilience — the ability to resist deterioration of these physiological variables as fatiguing endurance exercise proceeds — is the fourth dimension of performance.

Some athletes lose less than 1%. Others lose over 30%. Why?

The mechanistic basis for these differences is unresolved. The scientific community has poorly studied what happens beyond 90 minutes — let alone 4+ hours on mountain terrain. This is the gap.

Jones AM (2024). “The Fourth Dimension: Physiological Resilience as an Independent Determinant of Endurance Exercise Performance.” J Physiol, 602(17):4113–4128.
Jones AM & Kirby BS (2025). “Physiological Resilience: What Is It and How Might It Be Trained?” Scand J Med Sci Sports, 35(3):e70032.

What makes up resilience?

The Ingredients

If resilience is the fourth dimension, what are its components? That’s a difficult question that not Nike, not science, not anyone knows…yet. But we have our guesses, with unresolved evidence pointing to several interacting factors:

Muscular work capacity · Substrate availability · Neuromuscular fatigue resistance · Thermoregulation · Cognitive processing · Training history
